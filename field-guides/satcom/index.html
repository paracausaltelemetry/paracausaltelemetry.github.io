Orbits at a glance

OrbitHeightRound tripExamplesWhat it means for you
LEO340–1,200 km20–60 msStarlink, Eutelsat OneWeb, Iridium, Amazon KuiperFeels like broadband. Satellites move, so the link hands over constantly
MEO~8,000 km~150 msSES O3b mPOWERFibre-like throughput for remote sites and ships
GEO35,786 km600 ms or moreViasat (incl. Inmarsat), HughesNet, VSATFixed dish, slow round trip, TCP accelerators in the path
Bands
L-band (1–2 GHz): small antennas, rain-proof, low bandwidth (Iridium, Inmarsat BGAN). Ku (12–18 GHz) and Ka (26–40 GHz): high bandwidth, hit by heavy rain ("rain fade").
User terminal
The customer's antenna. Starlink's is an electronically steered phased array ("the dish").
Gateway / teleport
The ground station linking satellites to terrestrial fibre.
PoP
Point of presence: where your traffic actually reaches the internet, often hundreds of km from the dish.
Inter-satellite links
Lasers between Starlink satellites, so traffic can cross oceans with no nearby gateway.
Obstruction
Anything (trees, masts, superstructure) blocking the dish's view. Causes short outages.
Handover
Switching satellites. Starlink reschedules every 15 seconds, so expect brief latency spikes.
VSAT
Very small aperture terminal: a fixed GEO dish, common on ships, rigs and remote sites.

Starlink end to end

Starlink router
Wi-Fi, NAT and DHCP for the LAN (192.168.1.0/24 by default). Can be put in bypass mode.
Bypass mode
Turns off the Starlink router's routing so your own firewall takes the WAN directly.
Dish management IP
192.168.100.1, reachable from the LAN. Serves a local status page and an API with no login.
Starlink account
The web and app account that controls the service: plan, location, router settings. Protect it with MFA.
Roam / Maritime / Aviation plans
Mobile use. The terminal reports its location to SpaceX, and some plans are fenced to a country.
Priority / business plans
Better priority, and options such as public IPv4.

Addresses: what CGNAT does

Most Starlink plans give your router a carrier-grade NAT address, not a public one. Every packet is translated twice before it reaches the internet.

100.64.0.0/10
Shared address space for CGNAT (RFC 6598). On your firewall's WAN, it means "you are behind carrier NAT".
Shared public IP
Many customers leave from the same public IP. IP blocklists and reputation catch the innocent too.
IPv6
Starlink hands out a routable IPv6 prefix (typically a /56). Devices are reachable directly, so your firewall must deny inbound IPv6.
Public IPv4 option
On some business plans. Needed for inbound services without a tunnel.

Reverse DNS anatomy

Read: the PoP, not the dish, decides where an IP geolocates. A vessel in the North Sea can appear in London, Frankfurt or Amsterdam as it moves.

Hunt: sign-ins over Starlink (Entra ID)

kqlSigninLogs
| where TimeGenerated > ago(30d)
| where AutonomousSystemNumber == 14593
| summarize SignIns = count(), Ips = dcount(IPAddress), Countries = make_set(tostring(LocationDetails.countryOrRegion), 10) by UserPrincipalName
| order by SignIns desc

Hunt: sign-ins over Starlink (Okta System Log)

textsecurityContext.asNumber eq 14593 and eventType eq "user.session.start"

Hunt: Starlink sources in SecOps

udmprincipal.ip_geo_artifact.network.asn = "14593"
$user = principal.user.userid
match:
  $user
outcome:
  $events = count(metadata.id)
  $ips = array_distinct(principal.ip)
order:
  $events desc

Tune: check the enrichment field in the UDM viewer first. ASN fields only exist where geo enrichment has run.

A complex site behind a firewall

A remote site, vessel or branch with Starlink as the primary link, cellular as the second, and GEO VSAT as the last resort, all behind one edge firewall.

SD-WAN steering
Picks a WAN per application by latency, jitter and loss. Voice prefers Starlink, bulk sync waits.
Health checks
Probes per WAN. Tune for Starlink's short drops (loss over a few seconds), or you'll flap.
Session stickiness
Keeps a flow on one WAN. Moving an established session changes its public IP and breaks it.
Segmentation
Corporate, OT and guest on separate VLANs and zones. Guest traffic can leave locally; OT never should.
Local breakout vs backhaul
Send SaaS straight out (faster) or through HQ or SASE (inspected). Decide per zone.

Firewall checklist

RuleWhy
WAN1 on DHCP, expect 100.64.x.xStarlink CGNAT. Don't hard-code it
Static route 192.168.100.1/32 via WAN1, admin VLAN onlyDish stats from your network without exposing them
Deny all other LAN access to 192.168.100.1The dish API has no login: anyone on the LAN can read it, or reboot or stow the dish
Default deny inbound IPv6 on every WANStarlink IPv6 makes LAN devices globally reachable
Force internal DNS; block outbound DNS and known DoH elsewhereKeeps DNS logging and filtering when the link changes
Clamp TCP MSS on tunnels (about 1360)Avoids fragmentation and stalls over IPsec
Buffer logs locally, ship over the tunnelYou keep evidence through outages
Separate rule sets per WAN for guest trafficCrew or guest browsing never touches the corporate tunnel

GEO and VSAT gotchas

GotchaEffect
600 ms round tripChatty protocols (SMB, RDP over TCP, some SaaS) feel broken
PEP / TCP accelerationSplits TCP: speeds up plain TLS sessions, does nothing for IPsec or QUIC
Rain fade (Ku / Ka)Drops throughput or the link in heavy rain. L-band backup survives
Shared, contended capacityBusy-hour slowdowns. QoS at the edge matters more than raw speed
Metered data on L-bandA misbehaving update can cost real money. Block bulk traffic on that WAN

Maritime and remote operations

IACS UR E26 / E27
Classification society cyber rules for ships contracted from July 2024: network segmentation, asset inventories, secure remote access.
Fleet Xpress / Certus
Inmarsat Ka-band GEO with L-band backup, and Iridium L-band LEO. Common backup links alongside Starlink.
GNSS dependency
Starlink terminals need satellite positioning. GPS jamming or spoofing can degrade service, and it shows in navigation systems too.
Crew welfare network
Treat it as untrusted. It shares the dish, not the network.
